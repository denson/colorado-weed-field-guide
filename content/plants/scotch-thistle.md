# Scotch thistle

*Onopordum acanthium*

**Colorado noxious weed · List B.** [J-scotch-thistle](https://www.jeffco.us/2192/Scotch-Thistle)

> **Sharp spines; competes with vegetation.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Colorado also has native thistles. Purple flowers and spines alone cannot distinguish a listed introduced species.

## Three views of this plant

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

{{GALLERY}}

## Recognize

A large, woolly biennial with broad leaves, purple heads and conspicuous spiny stem wings. [J-scotch-thistle](https://www.jeffco.us/2192/Scotch-Thistle)

## Plants & habitat

Can form dense rosettes and persist through a long-lived seed bank. Maintain competing vegetation and prevent seed production. [J-scotch-thistle](https://www.jeffco.us/2192/Scotch-Thistle)

## People

Spines can puncture skin. Sturdy gloves are a practical precaution when handling; confirm identification before removing a thistle. [J-scotch-thistle](https://www.jeffco.us/2192/Scotch-Thistle)

*Evidence scope: Physical-risk inference from documented spines.*

## Dogs & cats

Spiny material can injure paws or mouths. Species-specific chemical toxicity is unresolved in this review. [J-scotch-thistle](https://www.jeffco.us/2192/Scotch-Thistle)

*Evidence scope: Physical-risk inference; toxicology gap.*

## Other animals

The reviewed control source does not establish species-specific livestock chemical toxicity. This is an evidence gap, not proof of safety.
