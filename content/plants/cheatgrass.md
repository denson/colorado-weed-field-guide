# Cheatgrass / downy brome

*Bromus tectorum*

**Colorado List C.** [S20](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/cheatgrass-and-wildfire/)

> **Grass awns · Fire hazard.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Three views of this plant

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

{{GALLERY}}

## Recognize

An annual grass with soft hairs and drooping seed heads. Often turns reddish-brown, then dries early in summer. [S20](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/cheatgrass-and-wildfire/)

## Plants & habitat

Competes early for resources, displaces native plants, and creates dry fuels that increase wildfire risk. [S20](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/cheatgrass-and-wildfire/)

## People

Dense, dry growth is highly flammable. The principal human hazard emphasized by the Colorado source is wildfire. [S20](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/cheatgrass-and-wildfire/)

## Dogs & cats

Barbed grass seeds can lodge in tissue and cause infection. Check fur and paws; seek veterinary help for persistent sneezing, head shaking, or an embedded seed. [S22](https://healthtopics.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/health-topics/foxtails)

## Other animals

CSU lists cheatgrass among plants that cause mechanical injury to livestock. Habitat displacement also affects wildlife. [S21](https://cmg.extension.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/42/2016/10/SourceUnitInRangeAndPastureMngmt_REVISED_5-22-2017.pdf) [S20](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/cheatgrass-and-wildfire/)
