# Lewis blue flax

*Linum lewisii*

**Colorado native; low-water garden plant.** [C-perennials](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/native-herbaceous-perennials-for-colorado-landscapes/)

> **Ingestion and contact concerns.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Use Linum lewisii records, not L. perenne or culinary flax L. usitatissimum. The source’s food-use language is not adopted as preparation advice.

## Recognize

Delicate five-petaled blue flowers above narrow leaves on slender stems. [N-linum-lewisii](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/linum-lewisii/)

## Plants & habitat

A short-lived perennial that reseeds freely; reduce seed set if many volunteers are unwanted. It needs sun and drainage. [N-linum-lewisii](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/linum-lewisii/)

## People

The source flags cyanogenic glycosides and contact dermatitis. This guide does not establish edible preparations or safe doses. [N-linum-lewisii](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/linum-lewisii/)

## Dogs & cats

Species-specific ingestion effects and exposure thresholds were not established in the reviewed sources. This is an evidence gap, not proof of safety.

## Other animals

Large intake is reported as a concern for cattle and sheep, with potentially severe respiratory effects. Do not assume ornamental flax is suitable feed. [N-linum-lewisii](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/linum-lewisii/)
