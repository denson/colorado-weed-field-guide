# Silverleaf nightshade

*Solanum elaeagnifolium*

**Native Colorado plant · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Toxic leaves and berries; persistent spreading roots.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Distinct from buffalobur, horsenettle and black nightshade species. Native status does not imply harmlessness or prevent it being invasive outside its native range.

## Identification

A perennial with silvery, hairy leaves, often prickly stems and showy violet flowers. Young root sprouts may look like seedlings but lack seed leaves. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/)

## People

Leaves and berries contain variable amounts of toxic glycoalkaloids. Do not eat this plant or use berry color as a safety test. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/)

## Dogs & cats

Keep pets from chewing this identified nightshade as a precaution. The reviewed source documents human and livestock toxicity, but does not establish pet-specific clinical effects or a safe amount. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/)

*Evidence scope: Precautionary inference; pet evidence unresolved.*

## Other animals

Leaves and berries can poison livestock. Consumption by wild birds or small mammals does not establish safety for domestic animals. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/)

## Plants & habitat

Deep roots and persistent growth can reduce pasture or crop production through competition for water and nutrients. CNHP treats the species as native in Colorado. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/) [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf)

## Management & timing

Distinguish seedlings from root sprouts and plan repeated monitoring of established patches. Seek local management advice for extensive infestations. [U-silverleaf](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weeds-identification-gallery/silverleaf-nightshade/)

*Evidence scope: Management inference from root persistence.*

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
