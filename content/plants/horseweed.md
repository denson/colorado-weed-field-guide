# Horseweed

*Erigeron canadensis*

**Native Colorado plant · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Fast windborne spread; some populations resist herbicides.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** CNHP treats Conyza canadensis as native in its 2020 assessment and records historical disagreement over western nativity. NC State uses Erigeron canadensis. Horseweed is also a common name for unrelated plants.

## Identification

Starts as a hairy leaf rosette, then rises into a narrow upright plant with many small pale flower heads near the top. Leaves become smaller up the stem. [N-erigeron-canadensis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erigeron-canadensis/)

## People

NC State reports mucosal irritation. Avoid ingestion; the source does not establish a safe amount. [N-erigeron-canadensis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erigeron-canadensis/)

## Dogs & cats

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for dogs or cats. Missing evidence does not establish safety.

## Other animals

The same source reports mucosal irritation in livestock. It gives no validated safe feeding rate. [N-erigeron-canadensis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erigeron-canadensis/)

## Plants & habitat

A prolific disturbance weed with windborne seed; flowers and hollow stems also support native bees. Glyphosate resistance is documented in some populations, not assumed for every Colorado patch. [N-erigeron-canadensis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erigeron-canadensis/)

## Management & timing

Remove plants completely before seed release; broken stems can regrow. Seek local advice when repeated treatment fails. [N-erigeron-canadensis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erigeron-canadensis/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
