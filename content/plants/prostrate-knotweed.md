**Identification caveat:** This small lawn weed is not Japanese knotweed or another large invasive Reynoutria species. Polygonum aviculare is treated broadly here. ## Identification

Look for wiry, low stems, alternating small leaves and papery sheaths around the swollen joints. Tiny flowers appear where the leaves meet the stems. [N-polygonum-aviculare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/polygonum-aviculare/)

## People

## Dogs & cats

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for livestock. Missing evidence does not establish safety.

## Plants & habitat

It tolerates trampling, poor soil and pavement cracks. A persistent seed supply helps it return where desirable vegetation struggles. [N-polygonum-aviculare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/polygonum-aviculare/)

## Management & timing

Mowing misses much of this low plant. Address compaction in affected turf and remove small plants before seed production. [N-polygonum-aviculare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/polygonum-aviculare/) [C-descriptions](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/weed-descriptions/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
