# Tall larkspur

*Delphinium barbeyi*

**Native Colorado wildflower; grazing and ingestion hazard.** [C-routt-guide](https://routt.extension.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2021/06/RC_Weed_Guide_2021.pdf)

> **Serious livestock and pet poisoning hazard.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** This profile focuses on Delphinium barbeyi. Other larkspurs differ in appearance and alkaloid content. Genus-level pet warnings are clearly marked; cattle exposure amounts are not transferred to pets.

## Recognize

Tall larkspurs bear clusters of blue spurred flowers above deeply lobed leaves. [ARS-larkspur](https://www.ars.usda.gov/pacific-west-area/logan-ut/poisonous-plant-research/docs/larkspur-delphinium-spp/)

*Evidence scope: Larkspur-group morphology; use a species key.*

## Plants & habitat

Routt County records tall larkspur on higher-elevation slopes and in aspen habitats. [C-routt-guide](https://routt.extension.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2021/06/RC_Weed_Guide_2021.pdf)

## People

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for people. Missing evidence does not establish safety. 

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists Delphinium species as toxic to dogs and cats. Alkaloids can disrupt nerve and muscle function; suspected ingestion needs prompt veterinary attention. [A-larkspur](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/larkspur)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level warning; no species-specific pet threshold.*

## Other animals

Larkspur is a major cattle-poisoning concern. All parts are toxic; severity varies with species, growth stage and conditions. [ARS-larkspur](https://www.ars.usda.gov/pacific-west-area/logan-ut/poisonous-plant-research/docs/larkspur-delphinium-spp/)

*Evidence scope: Larkspur-group evidence.*

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
