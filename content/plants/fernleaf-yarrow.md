# Fernleaf yarrow

*Achillea filipendulina*

**Introduced drought-tolerant perennial.** [N-achillea-filipendulina](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/achillea-filipendulina/)

> **Spreads; pet toxicity unresolved.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Do not merge this taxon with Achillea millefolium solely because both are called yarrow.

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

## Recognize

Tall stems hold flat yellow flower clusters above fern-like green leaves. [N-achillea-filipendulina](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/achillea-filipendulina/)

## Plants & habitat

Tolerates dry sites and can crowd its space; divide as needed and avoid overwatering. Cultivars differ in seed production. [N-achillea-filipendulina](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/achillea-filipendulina/)

## People

This species-specific horticultural source does not establish human ingestion safety. This is an evidence gap, not proof of safety.

## Dogs & cats

The ASPCA yarrow entry names Achillea millefolium. It does not resolve toxicity for A. filipendulina; prevent chewing while this gap remains. [S08](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/yarrow)
