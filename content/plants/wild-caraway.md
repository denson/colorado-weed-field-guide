# Wild caraway

*Carum carvi*

**Colorado noxious weed · List B.** [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Spreads by seed; pet safety concern.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** A white flower umbrella is not sufficient to separate caraway from poison hemlock or water hemlock; the comparison is an editorial identification precaution.

## Recognize

A biennial with finely divided, carrot-like leaves and small white or pinkish flowers in umbrella-shaped clusters. [N-carum-carvi](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/carum-carvi/)

## Plants & habitat

Self-sows in suitable moist sites. Culinary use does not remove its Colorado weed designation. [N-carum-carvi](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/carum-carvi/)

## People

White-flowered carrot-family plants require careful identification. The caraway description is not enough to rule out poisonous look-alikes. [N-carum-carvi](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/carum-carvi/)

*Evidence scope: Editorial identification precaution.*

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists Carum carvi as toxic to dogs and cats, with vomiting and diarrhea associated with its oils. No safe dose is established here. [A-caraway](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/caraway)

## Other animals

ASPCA also lists horses as susceptible. Culinary use by people does not establish forage safety. [A-caraway](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/caraway)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
