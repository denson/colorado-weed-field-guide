# Common cocklebur

*Xanthium strumarium*

**Common weed; not an A/B/C listing in the reviewed state rule.** [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Burs catch in fur; seedlings and seeds are hazardous.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Xanthium strumarium is treated broadly by some sources and split into other taxa by others. Photographs retain their original source identifications. Native status and a local specimen’s exact taxon need separate review.

## Recognize

A summer annual with rough, broad leaves and hard burs covered in hooked prickles. [N-xanthium-strumarium](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/xanthium-strumarium/)

## Plants & habitat

Can form colonies in fields and wet disturbed ground. Hooked burs move on animals and clothing. [N-xanthium-strumarium](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/xanthium-strumarium/)

## People

NC State flags human poisoning but provides primarily animal clinical descriptions. Do not infer a human dose or identical symptoms. [N-xanthium-strumarium](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/xanthium-strumarium/)

*Evidence scope: Source warning; human clinical detail limited.*

## Dogs & cats

Burs attach to fur and can cause contact problems. Animal ingestion warnings do not establish dog- or cat-specific thresholds; prevent chewing seedlings, seeds and burs. [N-xanthium-strumarium](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/xanthium-strumarium/)

*Evidence scope: Physical-risk inference and general animal warning.*

## Other animals

Seedling cotyledons and seeds are the principal ingestion concern. The source describes low blood sugar, weakness, spasms and potentially fatal poisoning. [N-xanthium-strumarium](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/xanthium-strumarium/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
