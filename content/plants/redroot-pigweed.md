# Redroot pigweed

*Amaranthus retroflexus*

**Common weed; not an A/B/C listing in the reviewed state rule.** [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Crop competition; nitrate and oxalate livestock risk.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Pigweed is used for several unrelated or similar-looking plants. Routt County’s prostrate pigweed entry concerns A. blitoides and is not used as exact A. retroflexus evidence.

## Recognize

An upright annual with hairy stems and dense green flower clusters; the lower stem or root may be reddish. [N-amaranthus-retroflexus](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/amaranthus-retroflexus/)

## Plants & habitat

A Colorado garden and field weed that competes strongly with crops and replenishes a seedbank. [C-descriptions](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/weed-descriptions/) [N-amaranthus-retroflexus](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/amaranthus-retroflexus/)

## People

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for people. Missing evidence does not establish safety. 

## Dogs & cats

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for dogs or cats. Missing evidence does not establish safety. 

## Other animals

Nitrate and oxalate accumulation can poison livestock. Conditions affect risk, so a plant’s appearance does not establish forage safety. [N-amaranthus-retroflexus](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/amaranthus-retroflexus/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
