# White locoweed

*Oxytropis sericea*

**Colorado native.** [F-locoweed](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/oxyser)

> **Toxic to livestock.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** The USFS synthesis dates to 1993; its taxonomy and cited studies are retained as historical evidence. No pet dose is inferred from livestock studies.

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

## Recognize

A low perennial with pinnately compound leaves and pale pea-like flowers; use a botanical key to separate similar locoweeds. [F-locoweed](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/oxyser)

## Plants & habitat

Native on open western plains and mountain sites, with persistent seed banks. Occurrence alone is not proof of an invasive-weed problem. [F-locoweed](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/oxyser)

## People

Human exposure effects were not established in the reviewed livestock sources; this is not a food plant recommendation. This is an evidence gap, not proof of safety.

## Other animals

Locoweeds can cause neurological damage, wasting and reproductive harm in livestock, including horses. White locoweed remains poisonous when dried. Keep it out of feed and seek livestock-veterinary advice. [F-locoweed](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/oxyser) [A-loco](https://www.ars.usda.gov/pacific-west-area/logan-ut/poisonous-plant-research/docs/locoweed-astragalus-and-oxytropis-spp/)

*Evidence scope: Species-specific persistence; locoweed-group clinical effects.*
