# Common purslane

*Portulaca oleracea*

**Common weed; not an A/B/C listing in the reviewed state rule.** [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Pet ingestion hazard despite human food uses.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Distinguish succulent purslane from low-growing spurges. The ASPCA entry is Portulaca oleracea, not merely an ornamental plant sharing a common name.

## Recognize

Low, spreading red-tinged stems, fleshy leaves and small yellow flowers. [N-portulaca-oleracea](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/portulaca-oleracea/)

## Plants & habitat

A familiar Colorado garden weed. Seeds and surviving stem pieces can sustain a patch. [C-descriptions](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/weed-descriptions/) [N-portulaca-oleracea](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/portulaca-oleracea/)

## People

The species has documented human food uses, but this guide provides neither a foraging identification nor a safe serving amount. Do not transfer food-use claims to pets. [N-portulaca-oleracea](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/portulaca-oleracea/)

*Evidence scope: Food-use record, not individual safety assessment.*

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists this exact species as toxic to dogs and cats because of soluble oxalates. Contact a veterinarian after suspected ingestion. [A-purslane](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/purslane)

## Other animals

ASPCA also lists horses. No safe forage amount is established here. [A-purslane](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/purslane)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
