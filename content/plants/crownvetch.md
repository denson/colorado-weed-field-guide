# Crownvetch

*Securigera varia*

**Introduced weed · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Toxic to horses; spreading roots can smother vegetation.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Older NC State material uses Coronilla varia; the Colorado assessment uses Securigera varia. This is separate from Vicia species and their distinct feeding hazards.

## Identification

A trailing perennial legume with many paired leaflets and rounded clusters of pink-and-white pea flowers. Unlike true vetches, it lacks tendrils. [N-securigera-varia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/securigera-varia/) [F-securigera](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/secvar)

## People

NC State lists poisonous characteristics and potentially serious ingestion effects. No safe amount is established. [N-securigera-varia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/securigera-varia/)

## Dogs & cats

Sources warn about nonruminants, but reviewed species-specific dog and cat clinical evidence is incomplete. Prevent chewing; cattle forage use cannot be treated as pet clearance. [N-securigera-varia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/securigera-varia/) [F-securigera](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/secvar)

*Evidence scope: Animal-group evidence; pet-specific gap.*

## Other animals

Crownvetch contains nitroglycosides toxic to horses. Its use as forage for some ruminants does not make it suitable horse feed. [F-securigera](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/secvar)

## Plants & habitat

It spreads by rhizomes and seed and can form a dense cover that displaces other plants. Former erosion-control use explains many naturalized colonies. [N-securigera-varia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/securigera-varia/)

## Management & timing

Avoid introducing it near natural habitat or horse forage. Established root systems need a sustained local control plan and monitoring. [N-securigera-varia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/securigera-varia/) [F-securigera](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/secvar)

*Evidence scope: Management synthesis.*

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
