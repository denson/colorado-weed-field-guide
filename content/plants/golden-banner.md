# Golden banner

*Thermopsis divaricarpa*

**Colorado native.** [C-perennials](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/native-herbaceous-perennials-for-colorado-landscapes/)

> **May poison livestock.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Do not silently substitute Thermopsis montana or T. lupinoides studies for T. divaricarpa.

## Recognize

Upright spikes of yellow, pea-like flowers on a native perennial. [C-perennials](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/native-herbaceous-perennials-for-colorado-landscapes/)

## Plants & habitat

Spreads vigorously by rhizomes. Give it room in a garden; its ability to crowd smaller neighbors is a garden-management concern. [C-perennials](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/native-herbaceous-perennials-for-colorado-landscapes/)

## Other animals

CSU Extension’s Boulder County livestock guide lists this exact species among native plants that may be poisonous to livestock. No safe dose or safe growth stage is established here. [C-banner](https://boulder.extension.colostate.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2021/03/BoCo-Rural-Living-Guide-Agriculture-Livestock-Rev-1.pdf)
