# Western whorled milkweed

*Asclepias subverticillata*

**Colorado native.** [S06](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/managing-western-whorled-milkweed/)

> **High livestock toxicity.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Recognize

Slender upright stems, very narrow leaves in rings of 3–5, and greenish-white flower clusters. Common at lower Colorado elevations. [S06](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/managing-western-whorled-milkweed/)

## Plants & habitat

Spreads by roots and seed into gardens and pasture. Also hosts monarch and queen butterfly caterpillars. [S06](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/managing-western-whorled-milkweed/)

## People

Avoid ingestion and sap contact. Human precautions here come from milkweed guidance for the genus, not a species-specific human study. [S05](https://www.poison.org/articles/milkweed-can-cause-serious-poisoning-204)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level.*

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists milkweeds as toxic to dogs and cats. This source does not give a safe amount for this species. [S04](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/milkweed)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level.*

## Other animals

Highly toxic to livestock. Dry plants remain hazardous in hay; a butterfly benefit does not make it suitable near grazing animals. [S06](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/managing-western-whorled-milkweed/)
