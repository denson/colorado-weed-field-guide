# Common teasel

*Dipsacus fullonum*

**Colorado noxious weed · List B.** [J-teasel](https://www.jeffco.us/2152/Teasel)

> **Prickly heads; prolific seed spread.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** The Colorado county designation is for Dipsacus spp. This page illustrates source-identified D. fullonum; cutleaf teasel, D. laciniatus, requires a separate identification.

## Three views of this plant

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

{{GALLERY}}

## Recognize

Prickly oval heads above stems with paired leaves that form cups. The county’s teasel account describes purple or white flowers and curved bracts. [J-teasel](https://www.jeffco.us/2152/Teasel)

*Evidence scope: Dipsacus group; source-identified photo examples.*

## Plants & habitat

Introduced teasels spread by seed in disturbed areas and ditch banks. Remove rosettes or flowering heads before seed dispersal and monitor for new plants. [J-teasel](https://www.jeffco.us/2152/Teasel)

*Evidence scope: Dipsacus group.*

## People

Prickly heads and stems present a handling hazard; gloves are a practical precaution. [J-teasel](https://www.jeffco.us/2152/Teasel)

*Evidence scope: Physical-risk inference.*

## Dogs & cats

Prickly dried material may injure paws or mouths. Chemical toxicity has not been established here. [J-teasel](https://www.jeffco.us/2152/Teasel)

*Evidence scope: Physical-risk inference; toxicity gap.*

## Other animals

Species-specific ingestion effects and exposure thresholds were not established in the reviewed sources. This is an evidence gap, not proof of safety.
