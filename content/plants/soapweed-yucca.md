# Soapweed yucca

*Yucca glauca*

**Great Plains native.** [S09](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/yucca-glauca/)

> **Sharp leaves · Toxic to pets.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Three views of this plant

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

{{GALLERY}}

## Recognize

A rosette of narrow, pointed leaves with upright stalks of pale, downward-facing flowers. Suited to dry, rocky soil. [S09](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/yucca-glauca/)

## Plants & habitat

Can expand through offsets and rhizomes. Garden judgment: allow room for the colony and sharp foliage. [S09](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/yucca-glauca/)

## People

Needle-sharp leaf tips are a physical hazard. Human ingestion safety is not assessed here. [S09](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/yucca-glauca/)

*Evidence scope: Species-specific physical feature; risk inference.*

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists Yucca species as toxic; vomiting is a reported sign. The evidence is for the genus, not an individual cultivar. [S10](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/yucca)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level.*

## Other animals

ASPCA reports liver disease and dermatitis in horses exposed to yucca. Yucca glauca also supports yucca moths and wildlife habitat. [S10](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/yucca) [S09](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/yucca-glauca/)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level toxicity; species-specific benefit.*
