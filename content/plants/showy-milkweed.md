# Showy milkweed

*Asclepias speciosa*

**Colorado native.** [S02](https://pueblo.extension.colostate.edu/low-water-native-plants-for-pollinators/) [S28](https://pueblo.extension.colostate.edu/loving-our-native-plants/)

> **Toxic if eaten.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Recognize

Pink flower clusters on an upright perennial; included in the Front Range native plant guide. [S03](https://www.extension.colostate.edu/docs/pubs/native/FrontRangeSm.pdf)

## Plants & habitat

Self-seeds and supports monarch caterpillars. Garden judgment: give volunteers room or manage seedlings beside smaller plants. [S02](https://pueblo.extension.colostate.edu/low-water-native-plants-for-pollinators/)

## People

Milkweed ingestion can cause serious poisoning; sap may irritate skin and eyes. Wear gloves when handling. [S05](https://www.poison.org/articles/milkweed-can-cause-serious-poisoning-204)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level.*

## Dogs & cats

Milkweeds are toxic to both. Signs can include vomiting and weakness, with potentially severe effects. Contact a veterinarian promptly after suspected ingestion. [S04](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/milkweed)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level.*

## Other animals

Milkweeds can poison horses and other livestock, while monarch caterpillars use this species as food. [S04](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/milkweed) [S02](https://pueblo.extension.colostate.edu/low-water-native-plants-for-pollinators/)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level toxicity; species-specific benefit.*
