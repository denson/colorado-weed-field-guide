# Green foxtail

*Setaria viridis*

**Common introduced weed · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Identify the grass behind the name “foxtail”.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** Setaria viridis is distinct from foxtail barley (Hordeum jubatum), giant foxtail (Setaria faberi) and yellow foxtail (Setaria pumila).

## Identification

A summer annual grass with narrow, bristly, cylindrical seedheads. Several bristles surround each small spikelet. It commonly grows in disturbed ground and lawns. [N-setaria-viridis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/setaria-viridis/)

## People

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for people. Missing evidence does not establish safety.

## Dogs & cats

The botanical sources describe bristly seedheads but do not establish the injury rate or ingestion toxicity of this species in pets. Avoid applying foxtail barley or cheatgrass exposure findings to every grass called foxtail. [N-setaria-viridis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/setaria-viridis/)

## Other animals

Grazing animals and small mammals eat this grass, but that observation does not provide a safe feeding amount or prove that mature seedheads are harmless. [N-setaria-viridis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/setaria-viridis/)

## Plants & habitat

It produces abundant seed and can spread rapidly on disturbed sites. Dense established vegetation is less vulnerable than open, poor-quality ground. [N-setaria-viridis](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/setaria-viridis/)

## Management & timing

Repair bare lawn patches and prevent seed production. A mowed plant may still form seedheads, so inspect close to the ground. [C-descriptions](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/weed-descriptions/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
