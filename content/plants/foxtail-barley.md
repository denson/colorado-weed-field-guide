# Foxtail barley

*Hordeum jubatum*

**Colorado native.** [C-foxtail](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/foxtail-barley/)

> **Barbed seed awns injure animals.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Recognize

A short-lived perennial bunchgrass with silky, often pink-tinged seedheads. It commonly occupies moist, disturbed or saline sites. [C-foxtail](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/foxtail-barley/)

## Plants & habitat

Can increase under heavy grazing as livestock avoid mature seedheads. Management should reduce seed production and support desirable grasses. [C-foxtail](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/foxtail-barley/)

## People

Sharp seed awns can catch on clothing; avoiding direct contact is a physical-risk precaution. This is not a chemical-poison diagnosis. [C-foxtail](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/foxtail-barley/)

*Evidence scope: Editorial physical-risk inference.*

## Dogs & cats

Grass awns can lodge in tissue and migrate, causing serious injury. Avoid mature seedheads and seek veterinary assessment for suspected embedded awns. The pet source discusses foxtails broadly. [S22](https://healthtopics.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/health-topics/foxtails)

*Evidence scope: Grass-awn group; not a species-specific pet case series.*

## Other animals

Mature awns injure livestock mouths, noses and eyes. Contaminated hay is unsafe and should not be fed or sold. [C-foxtail](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/foxtail-barley/)
