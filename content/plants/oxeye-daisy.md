# Oxeye daisy

*Leucanthemum vulgare*

**Colorado noxious weed · List B.** [J-oxeye-daisy](https://www.jeffco.us/2340/Oxeye-Daisy)

> **Toxic to dogs and cats; dermatitis.** Native status, garden use and noxious-weed class are separate from toxicity. [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** ASPCA’s page titled Daisy names Chrysanthemum species, not Leucanthemum vulgare. It is excluded as direct pet evidence; the species-specific NC State account supports the warning here.

## Three views of this plant

Photos are source-identified examples, not a complete identification key. Check leaves, stems, flowers and fruit together. Locations are not assumed to be in Colorado.

## Recognize

White ray flowers around a yellow center, with toothed or lobed leaves; a perennial that spreads through rhizomes. [N-leucanthemum-vulgare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/leucanthemum-vulgare/)

## Plants & habitat

Introduced ornamental that can spread in meadows and disturbed areas. Prevent seed production and manage the perennial root system. [J-oxeye-daisy](https://www.jeffco.us/2340/Oxeye-Daisy)

## People

NC State lists contact dermatitis from leaves and flowers. This guide does not assess edible use. [N-leucanthemum-vulgare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/leucanthemum-vulgare/)

## Dogs & cats

NC State reports ingestion toxicity, with possible vomiting, diarrhea, drooling, incoordination and dermatitis. Contact a veterinarian after suspected ingestion. [N-leucanthemum-vulgare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/leucanthemum-vulgare/)

## Other animals

The source flags horses but does not provide a horse-specific exposure assessment; no safe feed amount is established here. [N-leucanthemum-vulgare](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/leucanthemum-vulgare/)

## Before taking action

Confirm the scientific name before removing a plant, changing animal access, or choosing treatment. An evidence gap means this review has not resolved the question. It does not mean safe.
