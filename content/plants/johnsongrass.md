# Johnsongrass

*Sorghum halepense*

**Colorado noxious weed · List C.** [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Livestock cyanide risk; added fire fuel.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

## Recognize

A tall perennial grass with broad blades, pale midribs and a large, open seedhead. [N-sorghum-halepense](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/sorghum-halepense/)

## Plants & habitat

Rhizomes and seed form dense colonies. Productive stands can add fine fuels and promote fire spread. [N-sorghum-halepense](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/sorghum-halepense/)

## People

Pollen can trigger hay fever. This is not a substitute for grain sorghum. [N-sorghum-halepense](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/sorghum-halepense/)

## Dogs & cats

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for dogs or cats. Missing evidence does not establish safety. 

## Other animals

Young growth and foliage stressed by frost or hot, dry weather can contain harmful cyanide levels for cattle and horses. Obtain livestock and forage-testing advice before use. [N-sorghum-halepense](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/sorghum-halepense/)

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
