# Common blue violet

*Viola sororia*

**Native Colorado plant · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **A native volunteer; identify the species before removing.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** This is one member of CSU’s broader “Viola spp.” topic, not coverage of every Colorado violet. CNHP includes Viola sororia as native; photos and the profile do not resolve all members of the species complex.

## Identification

Basal, heart-shaped leaves and purple to white spring flowers arise on separate stalks. Later closed flowers can also produce seed. [N-viola-sororia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/viola-sororia/)

## People

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for people. Missing evidence does not establish safety.

## Dogs & cats

The reviewed sources do not establish species-specific ingestion effects or a safe exposure amount for dogs or cats. Missing evidence does not establish safety.

## Other animals

Rabbits browse the plant, and birds and small mammals use its seeds. Wildlife use does not establish domestic-animal feeding safety. [N-viola-sororia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/viola-sororia/)

## Plants & habitat

Rhizomes and self-seeding allow it to spread in moist, fertile sites. It also supports bees and butterflies and may be welcome groundcover. [N-viola-sororia](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/viola-sororia/)

## Management & timing

Confirm identity and decide whether a native patch can remain. For an unwanted lawn colony, CSU suggests removal when soil is moist; its advice covers wild violets as a group. [C-descriptions](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/weed-descriptions/)

*Evidence scope: Viola group management.*

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
