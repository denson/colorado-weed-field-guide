# Tall buttercup

*Ranunculus acris*

**Introduced weed · not on state Lists A/B/C.** [C-floristic-2020](https://cnhp.colostate.edu/download/documents/2020/CO_FQA_2020_Final_Report.pdf) [CO-rules-2025](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11948&fileName=8+CCR+1206-2)

> **Fresh plant irritation and animal poisoning hazard.** [Exposure help]({{BASE}}/safety/).

**Identification caveat:** The pet warning is genus-level evidence. It does not establish identical toxicity across every Ranunculus or justify blanket removal of native buttercups.

## Identification

An upright perennial of moist meadows and disturbed ground with glossy yellow flowers. Confirm the species: Colorado has many native buttercups as well. [N-ranunculus-acris](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ranunculus-acris/)

## People

NC State flags poisonous characteristics but presents livestock effects in its symptom field. Human exposure severity and a safe amount remain unresolved here. [N-ranunculus-acris](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ranunculus-acris/)

*Evidence scope: Source-field limitation.*

## Dogs & cats

ASPCA lists Ranunculus species as toxic to pets through an irritant toxin. Reported group-level signs include drooling, oral ulcers, vomiting, diarrhea and unsteady movement. [P-buttercup](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/buttercup)

*Evidence scope: Genus-level veterinary evidence.*

## Other animals

NC State reports mouth injury, diarrhea and potentially severe systemic effects in livestock ingesting this species. [N-ranunculus-acris](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ranunculus-acris/)

## Plants & habitat

A nonnative perennial that grows in moist meadows, pastures and roadsides. Its flowers also attract pollinating insects. [N-ranunculus-acris](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ranunculus-acris/)

## Management & timing

Confirm identity and prevent animal access to suspect plants or contaminated forage. Choose control for the site rather than treating every yellow buttercup as this species. [N-ranunculus-acris](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ranunculus-acris/) [P-buttercup](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/buttercup)

*Evidence scope: Management synthesis.*

## Before taking action

Confirm identification and the requirements of your county weed program. For chemical treatment, follow the current product label, including restrictions around animals and water. [S27](https://planttalk.colostate.edu/topics/weeds-cultural-problems/2103-colorado-noxious-weeds/)
